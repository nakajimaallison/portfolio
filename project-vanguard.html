CBS Sports: Bringing the energy of sports into the UI

A systems redesign that transforms flat screens into dynamic experiences.

In development
Hero Image

Context & Challenge

When I joined CBS Sports in 2023, the design system was struggling to keep pace with the rapid growth of digital products. Multiple teams were creating variations of the same components, leading to inconsistent user experiences across platforms.

The core issues we identified were:

  • Outdated component library with no version control
  • Minimal documentation for designers and developers
  • No standardized handoff process between teams
  • Significant design debt accumulated over 3+ years
  • Teams spending more time on component creation than product work

The challenge was clear: we needed to modernize the system while maintaining backward compatibility and getting buy-in from stakeholders across the organization.

Research & Discovery

I started by conducting a comprehensive audit of our existing components and interviewing stakeholders across 8 product teams to understand their pain points.

Key Findings

"We spend 60% of our time recreating components that should already exist in the system. It's incredibly frustrating."

— Product Designer, CBS Fantasy Sports

Through stakeholder interviews and analytics review, we discovered that teams were most impacted by:

  • Unclear component usage: 73% of designers reported not knowing when to use certain components
  • Missing states and variants: Critical interaction states were missing from 60% of components
  • Poor accessibility: Only 40% of components met WCAG 2.1 AA standards
  • Handoff friction: Developers spent an average of 2 hours per component clarifying specifications

Design Process

With clear problems identified, I developed a phased approach to rebuilding the design system without disrupting ongoing product work.

Phase 1: Foundation & Architecture

I started by establishing the foundational elements—color systems, typography scales, spacing tokens, and grid structures. These would serve as the building blocks for all components.

Working closely with our engineering team, we ensured that every design token had a corresponding code variable, creating true design-development parity.

Phase 2: Component Library

Next, I rebuilt core components with a focus on flexibility and accessibility. Each component was designed with:

  • All interaction states (default, hover, active, focus, disabled, error)
  • Multiple size variants to accommodate different use cases
  • Built-in accessibility features and ARIA labels
  • Comprehensive documentation with usage guidelines
  • Real-world examples from existing CBS products

I prioritized components based on usage frequency and impact, starting with buttons, form fields, cards, and navigation elements.

Phase 3: Documentation & Training

Each component included usage guidelines, accessibility notes, do's and don'ts, and code snippets. I also created an onboarding guide for new team members and conducted live training sessions for all 8 product teams.

The introduction of Figma's Dev Mode was a game-changer. I developed a standardized workflow that reduced back-and-forth between designers and developers by 78%.

Results & Impact

The redesigned design system launched in Q1 2024 and was adopted across all CBS Sports products within 6 months. The results exceeded our initial goals:

78% Reduction in design handoff time
2,600+ User tests validating changes
95% Team adoption rate within 6 months
100% WCAG 2.1 AA compliance across components

Beyond the metrics, the qualitative feedback from teams was overwhelmingly positive. Designers reported feeling more confident in their work, and developers appreciated the clarity of specifications.

Key Learnings

This project reinforced several important lessons about design systems:

1. Start with people, not pixels

The most successful aspect of this project wasn't the components themselves—it was getting buy-in from stakeholders early and involving them throughout the process. Design systems are as much about culture change as they are about design.

2. Documentation is a product feature

We initially underestimated the importance of documentation. Once we invested in comprehensive guidelines and examples, adoption skyrocketed. Good documentation turns a component library into a true design system.

3. Design for the 80% use case

Early on, I tried to account for every possible edge case, which led to overly complex components. We learned to design for the most common scenarios and provide guidance for customization when needed.

A design system should make the right thing the easy thing.

Next Steps

While the design system has been successfully adopted, our work continues. Current priorities include:

  • Expanding the component library to include more complex patterns like data visualizations and interactive dashboards
  • Developing a contribution model so teams can propose new components
  • Creating animated motion guidelines to ensure consistent transitions across products
  • Building automated testing to catch design-development inconsistencies

The goal is to evolve the system alongside our products while maintaining the consistency and quality standards we've established.
